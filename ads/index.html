How a brief becomes a film

This is the actual order I work in. The expensive steps sit at the end on purpose, so a bad idea dies while it is still cheap.

  1. 01

    Script and shot list

    The film is broken into numbered shots with durations before a single frame is generated. Dhaga was 44 entries in a shot list before it was anything else.

    shots.json · 44 entries
  2. 02

    Character sheets

    Every recurring character gets a turnaround (front, side, back, close) locked before production. This is the step most AI work skips, and it is why most AI work drifts.

    4 characters, locked
  3. 03

    Canary the cheapest shot

    One hard shot gets generated first, at the lowest setting that still shows whether the look survives. If the canary fails, the prompt architecture is wrong and nothing expensive has been spent yet.

    1 test clip
  4. 04

    Batch generation

    Shots go out in batches rather than one at a time, through scripts I wrote against the tool, with resume and retry so a dropped job does not cost a whole run.

    22 batches · 9 tools
  5. 05

    Cull hard

    Anything with a morphed face, a broken hand or a continuity break is killed rather than patched. On Dhaga, more frames were thrown away than kept.

    51 stills and 12 clips rejected
  6. 06

    Repaint and assemble

    Survivors get targeted fixes, then the cut, sound and grade. The film is edited like a film, not assembled like a slideshow.

    final master

What that means on a brief

Range
Live‑action and generated, and the hybrid in between. I shoot plates and composite generated elements into them, so a spot is not forced to be entirely one or the other.
Cost control
I canary the cheapest version of the hardest shot before committing budget, and I batch the rest. The expensive generation happens once, at the end, against a locked look.
Tooling
I am a backend engineer by day. When the tool does not do what a production needs, I write the thing that does: batching, resume, retry, frame fetching, automated fixes.
Direction
The films on the other half of this site are live‑action, written and directed. Generation here is a camera, not the point.
